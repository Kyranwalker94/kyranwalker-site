Our Garden 🌱

Renovating a neglected garden in Hailsham, clearing, designing, and building it out, now into its first winter after a productive first growing season.

The project

After a few years of limited outdoor space, moving to a house with a proper garden in Hailsham in 2025 gave us the opportunity to actually use it. The garden came overgrown and underused, plenty of potential but in need of significant work before anything productive could happen.

The plan for 2026 was ambitious: clear what's there, design the space properly, build raised beds, and get as much growing as possible by the end of the season. The first season delivered well beyond what we expected for a garden that was overgrown wasteland a few months earlier, a huge haul of tomatoes, cucumbers, green beans, lettuce, onions, a little broccoli, two corn on the cobs, lots of different herbs, beautiful cosmos flowers and roses, plenty of strawberries, no shortage of jalapeños, a pumpkin that Amira has grown far too attached to (she won't eat it or carve it for Halloween), and aloe vera grown indoors.

The plan

  • Full clearance of overgrown areas, brambles, weeds, and unwanted growth
  • Layout planning, zoning the space for growing, seating, and paths
  • Raised bed construction, timber frames for vegetables and herbs
  • Soil preparation and composting setup
  • Grow list planning, prioritising crops that are expensive to buy or better fresh
  • Water butt installation for sustainable watering

Grow list 2026 (the plan)

The focus this year was on productive crops that justify the effort, things that are noticeably better home-grown, expensive at the supermarket, or just satisfying to produce.

  • Tomatoes, multiple varieties, both indoor and outdoor
  • Courgettes, prolific producers, easy to manage
  • Lettuce and salad leaves, fast growing, cut-and-come-again
  • Runner beans and French beans
  • Herbs, basil, parsley, chives, mint
  • Strawberries, long term planting for future seasons
  • Potatoes, if space allows

Over winter

With the main season done, everything else has been dug over and covered, ready for next year. Onions, garlic, and broad beans are now planted outside for autumn/winter, with spinach and the remaining jalapeño plant kept going in the greenhouse. The same onions, garlic, and broad beans would normally be going into the new allotment too, but that's being left to rest over its first winter instead.

The garden tool

Alongside the physical work, I'm building a digital companion tool to help plan and manage the garden. The idea is a digital twin of the real garden, a grid-based builder where you can map out beds, plants, structures, and paths to reflect what's actually there, and then maintain a separate "plan" version to experiment with layouts before committing to any real changes.

Once you're out of the editor, the garden comes to life in a 3D view, closer to something like Animal Crossing or Farmville than a dry spreadsheet. You can click on individual plants to read about them, set watering schedules, and track care tasks over time. The aim is to make it genuinely useful for day-to-day garden management, not just planning.

Once it's in a usable state I'm hoping to share it so that other people can use it for their own gardens too. It's being built alongside this project, so it's growing at the same pace as the garden itself.
